The chain

Three screens, always in this order

1Peer · 30 minA teammate audits your work. Everyone starts here — team leads included.
2Team Lead · 20 minAn assigned lead re-audits the same day, the same accounts.
3Manager approvalOnly after approval does the score reach your scorecard.

The system enforces the order. A lead cannot score before the peer has, and nothing counts until it's approved.

Narration: Every QA score passes three screens, always in this order. First, a teammate reviews you — that's the peer stage, thirty minutes. Second, an assigned team lead re-audits the very same work in twenty minutes. Third, the manager approves it. The system enforces this order: a lead cannot record a score before the peer has, and nothing reaches your scorecard until it is approved.

The accuracy rule

A careless review costs both of you

The team lead audits the exact same day and accounts. If they find problems you passed, the gap is charged to the reviewer and the person reviewed.

✓Within 3 pointsForgiven. Honest judgement differs — that's expected.
!Beyond thatDemerit points on both people, scaled to the gap, up to 2.0 each.
→Both are toldA notification names the gap and the reason. Nothing happens quietly.

Example: peer scores 96, lead scores 84 → gap 12 → 1.0 demerit point each.

Narration: Now the rule that matters most. The team lead audits the exact same day and the exact same accounts you did. If they find problems that you passed, that gap is charged to both of you — the reviewer for missing it, and the person reviewed for producing it. A difference of up to three points is forgiven, because honest judgement varies. Beyond that, demerit points land on both people, scaled to the size of the gap, up to two points each. For example: if you score ninety-six and the lead scores eighty-four, that is a gap of twelve, and you each take one demerit point. Passing everything to be kind is not kind — it costs you both.
