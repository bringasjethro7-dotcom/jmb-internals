JMB Huddle · Session 01

Using the QA Computer
accurately

How our weekly quality review works — who checks whom, what you are actually looking at, and the rules that keep every score honest.

Say: This session covers the QA Computer end to end. By the end you'll know exactly what to do when your assignment arrives.
The chain

Every score passes three screens

1Peer · 30 minA teammate audits your work. Everyone starts here — team leads included.
2Team Lead · 20 minAn assigned lead re-audits the same day and the same accounts.
3Manager approvalOnly after approval does the score reach your scorecard.

The order is enforced by the system. A lead cannot score before the peer has, and nothing counts until it is approved.

Say: Three screens, always in this order. Nobody skips a stage, and no score touches your scorecard until it clears all three.
Your assignment

You don't choose who you check

Every Monday the system shuffles each project team into a circle: you audit one teammate, and one teammate audits you. Never yourself. New pairings each week.

Where you see itA notification in your portal, and the blue banner at the top of the QA Computer.
What it says"Week 2 · you are auditing Ocampo, Felilou Joy (ALTR Media)."
DueFriday 6:00 PM. Reminders land Wednesday and Friday if it's still open.
Say: You'll be told who you're checking. You never pick, and you can't review yourself — the system won't allow it.
The most important rule

Audit one day, not the whole week

Audit this dayTuesday, Aug 5

The system picks one day that person actually worked, and everyone in the chain audits that same day — the peer, the lead, and the manager. Nobody can pick an easy day, and the person being reviewed can't know it in advance.

Say: Look at the red panel. That date is your scope. Not the week — that day only.
What you check

The accounts are assigned too

The columns are filled with the real accounts that person worked on the audit day, pulled from their tool. You can't add, remove, or rename them.

Reddit, Ember,
John, IG Reels
Real usernames appear as the column headers.
ALTR
Accounts come from the ALTR tracker, shown with the phone they sit on.
Say: Whatever is in those columns is what you audit. If a column looks wrong, tell an admin — don't work around it.
Doing the review

Press Start, then judge honestly

1 · Start reviewYour start and end time are recorded. Peer target 30 minutes, lead 20.
2 · Tick each criterion✓ pass, ✗ fail — per account. Click a row label to flip the whole row.
3 · Note what you foundUse the note box for context and recurring issues. This is what helps the person improve.

Everything is saved as you go. A refresh, a crash, or a dropped connection will not lose your work.

Say: Start the timer first — submitting is blocked without it. Then work through each account properly.
Accuracy policy

A careless review costs both of you

The team lead audits the exact same day and accounts you did. If they find problems you passed, that gap is charged to the reviewer and the reviewee.

Within 3 pointsForgiven. Honest judgement differs — that's expected.
Beyond thatDemerit points on both people, scaled to the gap, up to 2.0 each.
Everyone is toldBoth parties get a notification naming the gap and the reason.
Say: Passing everything to be kind is not kind. If the lead finds what you missed, you both take the hit.
Scoring

How the number is built

Score = checks passed ÷ total checks, across every account and task on that day.

Client's QA
A 10% deduction applied by the team lead or admin only — never at the peer stage.
Weekly → monthly
Four approved weekly scores average into your monthly QA, worth 20% of your scorecard.
Say: Your weekly QA feeds your monthly scorecard, and that's 20% of your overall score.
For team leads

You review a teammate first

Leads are peers too. Your lead queue stays locked until your own peer review for the week is submitted — the system checks, so there's no way around it.

Then your queue opensOnly the reviews assigned to you by the shuffle appear.
Same scopeSame audit day, same accounts the peer saw — that's what makes the comparison fair.
Say: Leads, do your own peer review first. The queue won't unlock until you have.
