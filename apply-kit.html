The five questions you will get

Have these ready. The ban question is the one you win on.
"What is your rate?"

Give a range with a reason attached, never a bare number. "Depends on accounts and daily volume — for [N] accounts at [X] posts a day I'd be around [rate]. Happy to price a test week lower so you can judge before committing."

"How many accounts can you handle?"

Honest and specific. Overcommitting is how the relationship dies in month two. Say what one person carries properly, and that you scale with vetted specialists under the same SOP past that point.

"Can you work US hours?"

Yes — name the hours in their timezone, not yours.

"How do I know the work is being done?"

Your strongest ground. Screenshot on every task, daily log per account, checklist scoring on a review cycle. Offer to show the reporting format.

"What happens if an account gets banned?"

Never claim it won't. "It happens to everyone in this niche. The difference is you hear about it from me the same day, the account comes out of rotation immediately, and a replacement or warm-up starts rather than the schedule quietly draining." Honesty here reads as experience, because it is.

What not to do

Don't open with "hardworking and passionate."

It's what every other applicant writes, and it signals you have nothing concrete to lead with.

Don't attach the spec portfolio.

Every metric in it is stamped SAMPLE. A buyer who reads that label then has reason to re-examine everything else you sent.

Don't follow up more than twice.

Two is persistence. Four is a reason not to hire you.

Don't invent history.

Buyers in this niche ask for handles and references as a matter of course. A backstory that collapses costs the client, the platform reputation, and every referral behind it.
