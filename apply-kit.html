💬 Interview answers

The five questions you will get

Have these ready. The ban question is the one you win on.
"What is your rate?"

Give a number with a reason attached. "Depends on accounts and daily volume — for [N] accounts at [X] posts a day I'd be around [rate]. Happy to start with a paid test so you can see the work first."

"How many accounts can you handle?"

Honest and specific. Overcommitting is how you lose the role in month two. Say what you can genuinely carry at their quality bar, and ask what daily volume per account looks like before committing to a number.

"Can you work US hours?"

Yes — name the hours in their timezone, not yours.

"How do I know the work is being done?"

Your strongest ground. A screenshot on every task and a daily sheet per account. Offer to show the format — most applicants have nothing to show here.

"What happens if an account gets banned?"

Never claim it won't. "It happens to everyone in this niche. The difference is you hear about it from me the same day, the account comes out of rotation instead of quietly draining the schedule." Honesty here reads as experience, because it is.

What not to do

Don't open with "hardworking and passionate."

It's what every other applicant writes, and it signals you have nothing concrete to lead with.

Don't attach the spec portfolio.

Every metric in it is stamped SAMPLE. An employer who reads that label then has reason to re-examine everything else you sent.

Don't follow up more than twice.

Two is persistence. Four is a reason not to hire you.

Don't invent history.

Employers in this niche ask for handles and references as a matter of course. A backstory that collapses costs you the role and your standing on the board.

Intro video script — 45 seconds

Most posts that ask for a video just want to hear you speak English and sound like a real person. Say it once, use the same recording for every application. Do not read it word for word — glance and talk.
Hi, I'm Jethro, from Laguna in the Philippines.

I do daily social media posting for creator accounts — mostly Reddit and Instagram. On Reddit that means posting to the right subreddits on a set schedule, seeding comments, and handling DMs. On Instagram it's a story, a feed post and a trial reel per account, every day.

The part I care most about is keeping accounts alive. I work to written rules — at least four hours between posts on the same account, join the subreddit before posting in it, never the same caption or image twice in a day. Most bans in this work are self-inflicted, and that discipline is what prevents them.

I screenshot every task I do and keep a daily sheet, so you always know what went out and from which account without having to ask.

I'm available full-time on your hours. Thanks for watching.
